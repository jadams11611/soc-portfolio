Phishing link clicked by a finance user

T1566.001 Phishing: Spearphishing Link. Email domain, Tier 1. Lab quality rating: Strong.

Proofpoint Email Security Gateway alerted after Martha Johnson, a Senior Accounts Payable Specialist with access to payment processing systems, clicked a URL in a flagged email at 09:42:17 UTC. The message came from payrollnoreply@acme-corp[.]com, a lookalike of the legitimate acmecorp.com. SPF failed and DKIM returned none, so the email was not sent from an authorized server.

The link, hxxp://acmecorp-secure[.]xyz/login, was flagged as suspicious and appears built to harvest corporate credentials. The subject line, "ACTION REQUIRED: Update your direct deposit information," fits BEC and payroll-redirect patterns. The user's role raises the stakes: she can authorize wire transfers up to $25,000 and update vendor banking details.

Kill chain stage is Initial Access. Given the spoofed domain, failed authentication checks, suspicious destination, and high-risk user, I escalated for immediate investigation. Next steps are a user interview and an authentication log review to determine whether credentials were entered and used.

Service account used for an interactive login

T1078.002 Valid Accounts: Domain Accounts. Identity domain, Tier 1. Lab quality rating: Strong.

Service account ACMECORP\SVC_SQLBackup performed its first-ever interactive logon (Type 2) at 03:02 UTC on FINANCE-WKS-007. Three things are wrong: the host (a workstation, not BACKUP-SRV-01), the time (03:02 instead of the 02:00 scheduled job), and the logon type (Type 2 instead of Type 4 or 5). The subject field shows ACMECORP\j.doe initiated the session, meaning a person typed the service account password, which is a credential-sharing violation and a PAM bypass.

Fourteen consecutive failed logons preceded the success, consistent with password guessing against this account. Pivoting to the authentication logs shows a related sign-in for the same account from a Tor exit node geolocating to Kyiv, far outside its normal Chicago-area activity. That sign-in used legacy SMTP authentication, which is not covered by Conditional Access, so MFA was never challenged.

Post-login activity shows reconnaissance and persistence: whoami /all, net group "Domain Admins" /domain, an attempted net use \\DC01\C$ (lateral movement to the domain controller, blocked), and creation of a scheduled task named "SQLBackupMaint." The password is 847 days old against a 90-day policy. The account holds SQL sysadmin on PROD-DB-01/02 and local admin on three hosts, so the blast radius is full production database compromise.

Recommendations: rotate the password immediately and disable the account, isolate FINANCE-WKS-007 and j.doe's session, hunt for the scheduled task across the fleet, review backups for shadow-copy tampering, and engage incident response.
